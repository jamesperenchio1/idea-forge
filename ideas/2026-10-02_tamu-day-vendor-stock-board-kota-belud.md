# TamuTadi — Saturday-Night "What's Coming to Tamu" Stock Board for Kota Belud Sunday Market Vendors & Kota Kinabalu Buyers

## The Hook
- Every Sunday about 600 vendors spread over a muddy field in Kota Belud, Sabah. Bajau horsemen, Dusun grandmothers selling tuhau, and Iranun weavers sell seasonal Borneo produce nobody can buy in a KK supermarket. Buyers driving 75 km from Kota Kinabalu go on rumour. Some Sundays the bambangan is there and some Sundays it isn't, and nobody knows until they've already paid for the petrol.
- TamuTadi ("tadi" is Sabahan slang for "just now") is a WhatsApp-first stock board. On Saturday evening vendors send a voice note or tap three emoji buttons ("bawa tuhau 5 kg 🌿"). By 9pm a public page shows what's coming and roughly how much, plus the Sunday 6–11am rain forecast for the field.
- The data nobody has: a week-by-week record of what actually reaches a Bornean native market, which works as a phenology calendar for wild foods and is valuable to food researchers, chefs and the Sabah Tourism Board.

The forecast shows why a plain "rain tomorrow 100%" weather app fails tamu vendors. Sunday 4 October shows 100% daily rain probability, yet the hourly breakdown says the trading window (6–11am) has only a 3–10% chance, and the rain arrives after noon when stalls are packing up. A Taginambur grandmother who sees "100% rain" on her phone on Saturday night may decide not to pick 10 kg of lemiding fern, which loses the sale and leaves the KK buyer with nothing to buy. The week after looks much wetter: 12.9 mm on 7 October, the start of the northeast monsoon transition. The real decision point is next Sunday (11 Oct), not this one.

The macro numbers matter differently. Malaysian inflation fell to 1.38% in 2025, so urban KK households are spending more freely on weekend "balik kampung" food trips. Meanwhile the rural share is shrinking every year (22.6%), so the people who still forage tuhau and bambangan are fewer and older. A board that guarantees buyers will turn up keeps those vendors' Sunday trip worth making.

## The Problem

At 5:30am on a Sunday, Puan Rosnah, 61, loads two rattan baskets of tuhau (wild ginger shoots that Sabahans pickle with chilli and lime) and a sack of dried bambangan slices onto a cousin's pickup in Kg. Taginambur. She doesn't know whether "Ah Fook", the KK restaurant owner who bought 4 kg from her three weeks ago, will come today, and he doesn't know whether she'll be there. He drove out last Sunday, found no tuhau because a funeral kept the Taginambur sellers home, and swore off the trip. By 10:30am Puan Rosnah has sold 1.5 kg to locals at RM8/kg and carries the rest home to rot. Her Sunday nets RM12 after the RM10 she paid for her seat in the pickup.

Tamu markets in Sabah (Kota Belud on Sundays, Donggongon, Tuaran, Kota Marudu, Penampang) run on habit and rumour. There's no stall map, no vendor list, and no prices. Vendors rotate with harvest, weather, church and mosque events, rubber-tapping days and family obligations. The current workarounds are personal WhatsApp contacts (buyers know 3–4 vendors at most), the "Tamu Kota Belud" Facebook posts that tourists put up *after* the fact, and a few Grab-for-produce resellers who mark up 60–100% and only carry what keeps. Nobody solves this because it's too small for e-commerce platforms (vendors sell RM60–250 a week, have no business registration, and many can't type Malay comfortably, only voice-note in Dusun) and too unglamorous for government smart-city schemes.

If nothing changes, the death spiral continues. Unreliable supply makes KK buyers stop coming. Low sales mean the younger relatives stop driving grandmothers in. The tamu turns into a tourist photo stop selling Chinese-made souvenirs and imported vegetables from Kundasang middlemen, and the wild-food knowledge that lived in that field (tuhau, lemiding, tarap, bambangan) stops having a market and stops being foraged.

## Who Uses This

**Primary user:** Dusun/Bajau/Iranun smallholder women aged 45–70 from villages within 30 km of Kota Belud who sell foraged and home-produced food at the Sunday tamu. They use a hand-me-down Android phone mostly for WhatsApp voice notes and Facebook, have a monthly household income of roughly RM900–1,800, decide on Saturday afternoon whether it's worth picking and travelling, and depend on a relative with a vehicle.
**What they do now (and why it sucks):** They pick blind on Saturday, hope the buyers who know them show up, and sell the leftovers to locals for less or let them rot.
**When they pay:** Vendors never pay. Their only cost is sending a Saturday voice note. The trigger to join is the first Sunday a KK buyer walks straight to their spot saying "I saw you'd have tuhau."

**Secondary user:** Kota Kinabalu buyers: owners of Sabahan-cuisine restaurants (hinava, tuhau sambal, bambangan pickle), KK home cooks who make a monthly drive, Brunei and Labuan weekend visitors, and KK hotel F&B teams looking for "local sourcing" stories.
**Why they care:** A 150 km round trip costs RM40–60 in petrol plus a whole morning, so knowing on Saturday night that six vendors are bringing bambangan decides whether they go.

**Who definitely won't use this:** Kundasang highland-vegetable wholesalers (they already run truck logistics on fixed contracts), tourists who just want buffalo photos and woven hats, and anyone expecting online ordering or delivery. This is a "know before you drive" tool, not a marketplace.

## Feature Set

### MVP — Week 1-3
- **Saturday WhatsApp check-in:** At 4pm Saturday the bot messages each registered vendor: "Tamu esok? Bawa apa?" ("Tamu tomorrow? Bringing what?"). They reply with a voice note (Malay/Dusun, transcribed and keyword-matched to a produce list) or tap quick-reply buttons for their usual 3–5 items plus "banyak / sikit / tiada" (lots / a little / none).
- **Public "Esok di Tamu" (Tomorrow at Tamu) board:** A mobile web page live by 9pm Saturday listing every product with vendor count and rough quantity ("Tuhau — 4 vendors, ~15 kg"; "Bambangan — 1 vendor, sikit"), grouped by the market area (wet side, dry-goods row, horse area).
- **Trading-hours rain strip:** An hourly 05:00–12:00 Open-Meteo forecast for the tamu field shown as a simple strip, so "100% rain today" doesn't scare off vendors or buyers when the trading window is dry.
- **Buyer "I'm coming for…" ping:** A KK buyer taps "Saya datang untuk tuhau" ("I'm coming for tuhau") and the vendors bringing it get a WhatsApp alert that night. This is the single thing that changes a vendor's picking decision.
- **Sunday 11am sold-out check:** One quick-reply ("habis / tinggal / banyak tinggal", i.e. sold out / a bit left / lots left) builds the sell-through history.

### Version 2 — Month 2-3
- **Wild-food season calendar:** Using accumulated weekly check-ins, show "Bambangan season usually peaks weeks 41–48" with rainfall overlays, a public phenology chart of Bornean wild foods.
- **Multi-tamu rotation:** Add Tuaran (Sunday), Donggongon (Thursday), Kota Marudu (Saturday) and Penampang so buyers can plan a weekly tamu circuit, and vendors who sell at several tamus check in once.
- **Ride-share board for vendors:** Kampung-to-tamu pickup seats (RM5–10) so a vendor without a relative's car can still come, matched to the Saturday check-in.

### Power User / Pro Features
- **Restaurant standing order:** A buyer sets "I want 3 kg tuhau every week when available." Vendors get a guaranteed buyer, and the buyer gets first call before Saturday's board is published.
- **Chef/researcher data export:** A CSV of weekly product presence, quantity bands and sell-through for UMS (Universiti Malaysia Sabah) food-science researchers, food writers and the Sabah Tourism Board.

## Technical Implementation

### Suggested Stack
WhatsApp is the only realistic channel. Sabahan rural women live in WhatsApp groups and won't install an app or log into a website. Buyers are urban and fine with a link.

**Chosen stack:** WhatsApp Cloud API bot (Meta, first 1,000 service conversations/month free) + Cloudflare Workers + D1 (SQLite) + a static, server-rendered public board page. It's cheap enough to run on a few dollars a month forever, with no app for anyone to install.

### Key Technical Decisions
1. **Quantity bands, not kilograms or prices:** Vendors won't (and shouldn't have to) commit to exact weights or prices, since tamu prices are negotiated face to face. "Banyak / sikit" is honest and quick, and it's all a buyer needs to decide whether to drive.
2. **Product dictionary with Dusun/Bajau aliases instead of free-text NLP:** Tuhau is also "tuhau", "tuhau jeruk", or "bambangan"-adjacent mishearings in transcripts. A curated alias list (~150 items, built with a Kota Belud community volunteer) plus fuzzy matching is more reliable than an LLM guessing Sabahan food names.

### Hardest Technical Challenge
Voice-note transcription of Sabah Malay mixed with Dusun words. Generic Whisper models will mangle "lemiding", "tarap" and "linopot". Mitigation: lean on quick-reply buttons for each vendor's usual items (85%+ of check-ins should be single taps), use voice only for unusual items, and have a human-in-the-loop "did you mean?" confirmation button. Transcripts that fail to match go to a volunteer moderator queue that also grows the alias dictionary.

**Moat:** In-person vendor trust built Sunday by Sunday through a Dusun-speaking local, plus a proprietary week-by-week wild-food presence dataset that grows more valuable every season and can't be scraped from anywhere.

## Risk Factors

1. **Adoption:** Older vendors may not reply on Saturday, leaving the board patchy. → **Mitigation:** Default to "usual items, probably coming" for vendors who've checked in three weeks in a row and say so on the board, ask a younger relative to be "check-in buddy", and keep the reply to a single tap.
2. **Trust/Cultural:** Publishing vendor names and quantities could invite middlemen to pre-buy and resell, or cause friction between neighbouring stalls. → **Mitigation:** Show only product + vendor count + market area by default; vendor name and contact appear only if the vendor opts in, and paid buyers can't bulk-reserve.
3. **Data:** Inaccurate check-ins (vendor says "banyak", brings nothing) make buyers stop trusting the board. → **Mitigation:** The Sunday sell-through check builds a per-vendor reliability score shown as ⭐ only to the vendor, with gentle nudges, and the board shows "check-in confidence" per product.
