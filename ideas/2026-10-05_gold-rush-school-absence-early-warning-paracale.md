# BantayLubog — Gold-Price & Dry-Spell School-Absence Early Warning for Child Compressor Mining in Paracale

## The Hook
- Gold hit **US$4,157.60/oz this morning — ₱8,372 per gram at spot** — and in Paracale, Camarines Norte, every jump in that number pulls a few more 13-year-olds out of Grade 7 and into flooded pits, breathing through a garden hose fed by a diesel air compressor.
- Teachers *already* see it first: the same boys go absent for 3–5 day runs right when gold spikes or the weather dries out. BantayLubog turns attendance sheets teachers already fill in into an early-warning signal, cross-referenced against live gold price and the 7-day rain forecast — so the child labor focal person shows up at the household *before* the first dive, not after the first drowning.
- Free to schools, funded by child-protection grants (ILO, UNICEF, EU-funded responsible-gold programs) and by gold refiners and jewelers who need audited, documentable "child-labor risk mitigation" evidence for their ASGM supply chain.

The numbers line up into exactly the risk window teachers describe anecdotally. The last week in Paracale was almost dry (≤3.4 mm/day), seas off the coast are calm at 0.58 m, and gold is sitting above US$4,100/oz — at ₱8,372/g spot, even a buyer paying a heavy discount makes a single good day in a pit worth more than a week of a parent's farm wages. That is the combination under which compressor and "banlas" (tidal/riverbank sluicing) work is most attractive and most likely to recruit children. The window closes around Oct 8 when the forecast flips to 28 mm of rain and >1 m waves — which means the absence spike, if it happens, happens *this week*, and nobody outside the school will notice.

The national figures explain why this stays invisible. The World Bank series for Philippine child employment hasn't had a fresh survey point since 2011 (9.0%), so policy runs on decade-old numbers. Meanwhile secondary gross enrollment fell 6 points in a single year (91.5% → 85.3%, 2023→2024). National averages can't tell you *which* barangay or *which* week; a teacher's attendance sheet can.

## The Problem

It's a Tuesday in early October at a junior high school in Barangay Malaguit, Paracale. The Grade 7 adviser marks four boys absent — the same four who were out for three days in August. Gold is above US$4,100. It hasn't rained properly in a week. She knows exactly where they are: in a flooded pit a few kilometers away, taking turns going under with a plastic hose in their mouths while an adult runs the compressor on the surface. Human Rights Watch documented exactly this in Camarines Norte and Masbate a decade ago, and it hasn't gone away. It's driven by the same two things her week's data shows: a high gold price and dry ground.

The structure makes sure nobody acts in time. Teachers report chronic absence through the DepEd LIS and SF2 attendance forms, but those are compliance records reviewed monthly or quarterly, and they flag dropouts, not *patterned* absence that tracks the gold price. DOLE's Sagip Batang Manggagawa and the municipal child labor focal person only respond to complaints, and a teacher filing a formal "child labor" complaint against a neighbor in a small mining town where everyone's family is in the trade is socially dangerous. In some households the pit operator is an uncle and the gold buyer is the barangay kagawad. So teachers do nothing formal. They have a quiet word with the parent at the next PTA meeting, by which point the boy has made ₱1,500 in a day and doesn't want to come back.

If nothing changes, the pattern repeats every dry spell and every gold rally. Children drop out at Grade 7–8, compressor diving causes drownings, pit collapses, and decompression-type injuries, and mercury amalgamation goes on in backyards where younger siblings play. Each new record gold price, and 2026 has had many, turns into another set of kids lost from school in Bicol.

The trigger to *start using it* is the first time the bot pings her on a Monday morning, "Gold +4% this week, 6 dry days forecast: watch-list learners at elevated risk," and her two flagged boys are absent by Wednesday. **Secondary user:** The Municipal Social Welfare and Development Officer / Local Council for the Protection of Children (LCPC) child labor focal person in Paracale and Jose Panganiban, plus DOLE Camarines Norte Field Office profilers.

**Who definitely won't use this:** Large-scale mining companies' CSR departments looking for PR dashboards, or national-level DepEd planners who want a province-wide BI tool. This is a weekly, hyper-local signal for two municipalities, not a national data platform.

## Feature Set

### MVP — Week 1-3
- **Messenger attendance tap-in:** Each adviser sends the bot their absent learners by private code (e.g. "7-Sampaguita #12, #19") in under 30 seconds. No names leave the teacher's phone; codes are mapped locally by the school.
- **Gold-week risk index:** Every Monday 6am, a computed score per municipality combining the weekly % change in gold spot in pesos (Gold-API × FX), consecutive dry days (Open-Meteo past + forecast precipitation <5 mm), and calm-sea days (Marine API wave height <1 m, for coastal banlas) is pushed to all enrolled teachers in Bikol/Filipino.
- **Patterned-absence detector:** It flags any learner code whose absences cluster in high-risk weeks (e.g. ≥3 consecutive absences while the index is "high", repeated in ≥2 windows) and adds them to the teacher's private watch list.
- **One-tap safe referral:** The teacher forwards a flagged code to the school guidance coordinator, who decides whether to escalate to the LCPC focal person. The teacher is never named to the household or to the barangay.
- **Weekly barangay heat list:** The focal person gets an anonymized count of flagged learners per barangay plus the risk index, as a Messenger message and a printable PDF.

### Version 2 — Month 2-3
- **Return-to-class tracker:** Logs when a flagged learner comes back after a home visit, scholarship, or 4Ps compliance check, so the program can measure what interventions actually work.
- **Buyer-price crowd signal:** Teachers and focal persons can optionally report the local per-gram buying price (anonymously, by barangay), so the index tracks what pit operators are actually paid, not just London spot.
- **Typhoon-suspension logic:** Uses PAGASA advisories and Open-Meteo forecasts to suppress false alarms during class suspensions, so storm absences don't count as mining absences.

### Power User / Pro Features
- **Supply-chain evidence export:** Aggregated, de-identified quarterly reports (risk index vs. flagged-learner counts vs. interventions) for refiners, jewelers, and responsible-gold certification programs that need documented child-labor due diligence for Philippine ASGM origins.
- **Replication kit:** A config template to spin the same system up in other ASGM areas like Masbate (Aroroy), Benguet (Itogon), Davao de Oro (Diwalwal), and South Cotabato (T'boli), with location-specific weather points and dialect message packs.

### Key Technical Decisions
1. **Learner codes, never names:** The server only stores section-local codes, and the code-to-name mapping stays on paper with the adviser and guidance coordinator. This keeps the app within the Data Privacy Act of 2012's data-minimization principle and protects children if the database leaks, which matters in towns where pit operators and local officials overlap.
2. **Rule-based index before any ML:** A transparent score (gold week-over-week change + dry-day count + calm-sea count) that teachers and LCPC officers can understand and argue with beats a black-box model trained on a few hundred absence records. ML can come later, once two dry seasons of labeled data exist.

### Hardest Technical Challenge
Separating mining absences from every other reason a rural Bicol kid misses school: fiesta, illness, typhoon suspension, harvest, fishing, lack of fare money. A naive detector will flood teachers with false flags, and they'll mute the bot within a month. **Mitigation:** Only flag *repeated co-occurrence* (absence runs that line up with high-risk weeks in at least two separate windows), suppress weeks with official class suspensions, let teachers mark a flag "not mining" in one tap so the detector learns per learner, and pilot with 5–8 advisers who already know which kids mine, so the first month of data can be labeled honestly.

## Risk Factors

1. **Regulatory / Privacy:** Handling minors' attendance data falls under the Data Privacy Act of 2012 and DepEd data policies, and a leak could expose families to stigma or retaliation → **Mitigation:** Codes-only storage, mapping kept offline at the school, a signed MOA with the Division, and a Privacy Impact Assessment completed before the pilot.
2. **Adoption / Social:** Teachers fear being seen as informants in towns where mining is the whole economy, and families or pit operators may pressure them → **Mitigation:** Referrals route through the guidance coordinator and LCPC, never naming the teacher; frame the program as "keeping learners in school" (scholarships, 4Ps compliance, feeding programs), not as enforcement.
3. **Data:** Gold spot is a proxy; local buying prices, the rainy-season calendar, and fiesta schedules may matter more → **Mitigation:** Add the crowd-reported local buying price in V2, let teachers label false flags, and recalibrate index weights after the first season with actual co-occurrence data.
